01 · DATA AND TOKENS

The tools and materials

Think of arriving at a workshop before building anything.

  • Datasets: explore the books in Wolne Lektury.
  • Tokenizers: see how text becomes numbered pieces. Use our saved tokenizer and explore how tokenizers learn.
  • Modal: learn to run tasks on cloud computers.

We also download and prepare the books for chapter 2. Training your own tokenizer is optional.

“Get to know the text, the tools and where we’ll work.”

02 · PRETRAINING

Learning the next token

Think of practising autocomplete while reading books.

Our small model starts with random weights: the numbers controlling its behaviour.

It predicts the next token, compares its prediction with the actual text, and adjusts those weights.

It gradually learns patterns in Polish text. It can still produce nonsense.

“Guess what comes next. Learn from your mistakes.”

03 · SUPERVISED FINE-TUNING (SFT)

Learning from correct answers

Think of studying with an answer key.

We switch to Qwen3.5-0.8B, an existing pretrained model.

We supply Polish driving-test questions and correct answers, such as “C.” Training makes those answers more likely.

We then check its performance on questions outside the training set.

“Here is the answer you should give.”

04 · REINFORCEMENT LEARNING WITH VERIFIABLE REWARDS (RLVR)

Learning from scores

Think of practising a game with a scoreboard.

In a separate experiment, Qwen3.5-4B tries to write six-word stories containing two requested words, with no repeats.

A program checks each attempt. Training makes rewarded answers more likely. Passing the rules does not guarantee a good story.

“Try an answer. I’ll check whether it succeeds.”

SFT learns from supplied answers. RLVR learns from scores for its own attempts.
